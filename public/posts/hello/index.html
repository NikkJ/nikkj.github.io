About

August 7, 2026 · 2 min

Rubezahl is where I publish malware analysis and breakdowns of public incidents — with a bias toward Latin America, which stays underreported in English-language threat intelligence.

Who writes this#

I’m Nikolas Jensen, a threat intelligence analyst based in southern Brazil. Since 2022 I’ve worked across more than forty client environments — finance, industry, retail, healthcare, government, oil and gas, maritime and land logistics — which means most of my time goes to the gap between what a report says an adversary does and what actually shows up in someone’s telemetry.

Day to day I work with MISP, Elastic, Wazuh, Any.Run, Ghidra, Maltego, Shodan and Censys. I have a BSc in Computer Science and a few published papers on container orchestration security.

What’s here#

  • Malware analysis — static and dynamic, with detection logic where it earns its place
  • Public incident breakdowns — what happened, what was defensible, what wasn’t
  • Detection engineering notes, mostly Elastic EQL
  • Occasional arguments about how intelligence gets produced and consumed

What’s not here#

No samples hosted on this site — hashes and MalwareBazaar links instead. Nothing sourced from client environments. Nothing derived from private or underground access. IOCs are defanged. Everything published here is TLP:CLEAR and comes from public reporting or from samples I obtained publicly.

The name#

Rübezahl is the mountain spirit of the Riesengebirge: unpredictable, rough on the arrogant, fair to people who deal straight. Also a nod to where my family came from before Santa Catarina.

Notes#

I write in English; Portuguese is my first language. Corrections on either the technical content or the prose are welcome.

I’m open to remote threat intelligence work with European teams.
